What does Brian Oney believe?

Meliorism 2.0 — The Philosophy

What is Meliorism 2.0 (the philosophy)?

Meliorism 2.0 is an applied philosophy developed by Brian Oney. It extends classical Meliorism — the tradition, rooted in John Dewey, William James, Jane Addams, and the Chicago School, that holds the world can be made better through deliberate human effort — and adds one operating move: subtract before you add.

The better version of a person, a business, or a system is already present. Strategy is precision removal of what conceals it. More often than not you reveal the better version rather than build it from scratch; once it's visible, building sometimes follows. That starting point is what makes Meliorism 2.0 a practical method rather than a mindset.

How is Meliorism 2.0 different from classical Meliorism?

Classical Meliorism holds that improvement is possible through deliberate human action. Meliorism 2.0, developed by Brian Oney, adds a method: precision removal of what hides the better version, rather than importing new frameworks or strategies from outside.

Most advisors and consultants bring a framework from outside and apply it, which produces borrowed clarity that fits the framework better than it fits the business. Meliorism 2.0 is after native clarity — the framework that emerges from what is actually happening inside the organization. The result tends to hold longer, because the business already knows how to run it.

What is the Michelangelo Premise?

The Michelangelo Premise is the practical claim, drawn from Michelangelo's idea that the sculpture is already in the marble and the sculptor's work is to remove everything that is not it. Applied to advisory work, it means the core logic of a successful business is already running — a way of winning, a pattern of connection, a distinctive capability the owner hasn't fully named.

Brian Oney's job as an advisor is to find that logic and stand beside the owner while they use it deliberately, at scale, and under pressure. It is one of the named constructs at the center of Meliorism 2.0.

What is Meliorist Group?

Meliorist Group is Brian Oney's research and applied philosophy practice, based in San Francisco — where the ideas of Meliorism 2.0 are tested against real organizations and real people, not just written about.

Its applied work currently includes AI-assisted training engagements (in beta) — concierge-style implementations and facilitations built for longer-lasting benefits and genuine institutional memory — and consulting on AI constraints and guidance that keep an organization's use of generative AI aligned with its values, its systems, and, above all, its people. Advisory Work

How does Brian Oney's advisory model work?

Brian Oney's advisory model is a growth-participation arrangement, not a monthly coaching fee. The starting retainer is $10,000, credited against a 10% share of the profit increase generated together. If a business grows from $100,000 to $200,000 in profit, the participation is $10,000 — fully covered by the retainer — and everything above that threshold is shared at the same 10% rate.

The structure is deliberate: it makes Brian's outcome and the client's outcome the same outcome. He is paid to grow the business, not to fill a calendar.

Who is the advisory work for?

Brian Oney's one-to-one advisory is for established business owners ready to move from roughly $100,000 to $200,000 in annual profit, or beyond. It fits owners who already have a working model, real intellectual property, and a team, and who want a seasoned advisor — compensated on results — in the room for the decisions that move the number: pricing, positioning, client selection, and offers.

If current profits are under $100,000, CTCSP (Cracking the Code to Success & Profits) is the right starting point. A higher-participation arrangement may also be possible for sub-$100K businesses that can budget for it.

What does the advisory work actually involve?

There is no curriculum, no modules, and no worksheets. Brian Oney stays in the room while clients make the real decisions — pricing, positioning, client selection, offers, and the leverage points most owners are sitting on without knowing it.

It is one sustained conversation, over months, with someone who has seen the pattern before and can name what you are standing on. The work emerges from your actual situation, drawn from the Meliorism 2.0 method: find what's already working, clear what's in the way, and use it deliberately.

What is CTCSP?

CTCSP stands for Cracking the Code to Success & Profits — a weekly group training program for business owners and sales professionals, co-hosted by Brian Oney and Scott Sylvan Bell, whom Jay Abraham has named among his most trusted implementation coaches.

The program covers sales psychology, influence, and preeminence, and is built to move participants from knowing strategy to implementing it. CTCSP is the recommended starting point for businesses with profits under $100,000, before one-to-one advisory. More at ctcsp.com.

Meliorism 2.0 — The Daily Briefing

What is the Meliorism 2.0 daily briefing?

The Meliorism 2.0 daily briefing, published by Brian Oney at meliorism2.com, is a short daily read for trainers, coaches, educators, and knowledge workers navigating an AI-shaped economy. Each issue surfaces what's relevant, names what it means for people whose work depends on the parts of human practice machines cannot replicate, and gets out of the way.

No motivational padding. No product pitches. Signal. It shares the name of Brian's philosophy because it applies the same lens: pay attention to what's irreplaceably human, and subtract the noise.

Is the Meliorism 2.0 briefing free?

The Meliorism 2.0 briefing is donation-based, not free. There are no paywalls — anyone can read every issue — but producing something worth reading every day is real work, and it is sustained by readers who find it valuable.

Support tiers are $4.99/month (Student / Tight Budget), $19.97/month (Sustainer), and $297 one-time (Founding Supporter). The goal is 100 Sustainers — the number that makes the daily work genuinely independent.

Who is the Meliorism 2.0 briefing for?

The Meliorism 2.0 briefing is for trainers and facilitators who need to understand AI's effects on learning and behavior change without becoming AI engineers; for coaches and advisors who want to speak intelligently to clients about what's shifting and what isn't; for educators watching their field reorganize in real time; and for anyone whose work is human-centered and wants to stay that way.

It assumes the reader wants to protect and sharpen the human parts of their practice, not automate them away.

Why does the Meliorism 2.0 briefing exist?

Brian Oney originally architected the Meliorism 2.0 briefing to feed his own brain — a daily research instrument for gathering the ideas, research, and perspectives that develop his expertise as a Meliorist 2.0. He needed that signal and couldn't find it, so he built it.

The noise-to-signal ratio in AI coverage is brutal: most of it is written for engineers, investors, or people who want to feel anxious — very little for the trainer who needs to know what this means for their next cohort, or the coach deciding whether to automate part of their practice. So Brian publishes it openly. The same feed that develops his thinking is now open to anyone whose work is human-centered and wants to stay that way.

What is "A Pension for the Trained-On"?

"A Pension for the Trained-On" is an essay by Brian Oney about AI training data and the people whose work gets absorbed into models without consent or compensation. It opens: "My daughter is graduating from college this spring with a degree in music therapy. In the next ten years, an AI will be trained on her body of work. Without asking her. Without paying her."

The piece argues for compensating the human creators whose labor trains AI systems, and is available on Substack.
